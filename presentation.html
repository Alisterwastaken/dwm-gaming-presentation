Mobile Gaming & In-App Purchase Behavior

Understanding player spending patterns, ad tolerance, and churn drivers among young Indian gamers to unlock monetization strategies for mobile game publishers.

Domain: Mobile Gaming Industry Data Warehousing & Mining — ISE 1

Course: DWM (Sem 5)  |  October 2026

🎮 77 Respondents
📊 21 Attributes
💰 Monetization Focus

Problem Statement

The mobile gaming market in India is booming, yet most publishers struggle with low conversion rates from free players to paying users. Only a small fraction of players make in-app purchases, and excessive ads drive churn. This study analyses survey data from 77 young gamers to identify spending triggers, ad tolerance thresholds, and churn patterns — enabling data-driven decisions on pricing, ad placement, and retention strategies.

Key Findings

Five data-backed insights uncovered from the survey analysis.

1. Cosmetic Items Dominate Purchases
Skins & cosmetics were selected by 57 out of 74 gamers (77%) as their primary purchase, far ahead of gems/currency (43%) and battle passes (38%). Aesthetic appeal ("Looks / style") was the #1 purchase trigger cited by 44 respondents (59%).
2. Ads Are the #1 Churn Driver
"Too many ads" was cited by 38 respondents (56%) as a reason for quitting. 20 players (29%) have already quit a game because of ads, and 16 (24%) would pay to remove them. Only 12% said they don't mind ads.
3. Bimodal Spending — High Spenders Exist
Monthly spend follows a bimodal distribution: 31% spend ₹1-100 (micro-spenders) while 34% spend ₹1000+ (whales). This "barbell" pattern suggests different monetization strategies for each segment.
4. Subscription Model Has Untapped Potential
While only 9% said "Yes" to a monthly subscription, a massive 47% said "Maybe". This "persuadable majority" represents a conversion opportunity with the right pricing and trial offers.
5. Social Play Drives Engagement & Retention
34% play with friends/squad and 32% play both solo and squad. "Friends stopped playing" was the 4th most cited quit reason (25%). Social features are critical retention levers.

Conclusion

Summary of our analytical journey and the role of data analytics in mobile gaming.

Summary

Our survey of 77 young Indian gamers revealed: the audience is highly engaged (avg. satisfaction 4.15/5), smartphone-based (75%), and willing to spend — 60.8% made real-money purchases in the last 3 months. However, ads are the #1 churn driver, and retention depends on social dynamics and fair monetization.

Analytics Contribution

Data analytics transformed raw survey responses into 5 actionable insights and 5 business recommendations. By applying EDA, visualization, and BI dashboarding, we demonstrated: Data → Insight → Decision → Value. The Looker Studio dashboard enables stakeholders to self-serve filtered views.

Key Takeaway

Mobile game publishers should shift from ad-heavy to cosmetic-first monetization, introduce tiered subscriptions targeting the 47% "Maybe" audience, and invest in social retention features. Players want to spend — publishers just need to give them the right reasons, not the wrong ads.
